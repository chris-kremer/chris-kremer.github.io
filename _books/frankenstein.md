Currently reading. The familiar outline of the plot hides a much stranger and more intellectually ambitious book than I expected.

## The surprise of the form

The narrative structure immediately caught me off guard. The novel begins with letters and then turns into a framed account told by Victor Frankenstein to the man who rescued him. The story reaches us through layers of narrators and testimony rather than as a straightforward sequence of events. It is a striking stylistic choice that I want to think about more.

## Creation followed by abandonment

Victor is enticed by knowledge, advances in chemistry, and discovers how to create life. But the decisive moral action comes directly afterward: he creates the being and abandons it.

The Creature's awakening reads like birth without parents, guidance, language, or background knowledge. It enters a world that is immediately hostile to it. Its gradual acquisition of language, literacy, and human concepts is especially well done; the novel uses the Creature to ask how a mind and a moral sense are socially formed.

## Appearance, exclusion, and responsibility

The book contains a powerful version of “pretty privilege.” The Creature is excluded because it is ugly and therefore cannot integrate into society, despite initially appearing gentle and capable of sympathy.

It blames rejection and isolation for its later violence. That explanation has real force without fully removing responsibility—the murder of Victor's brother is presented almost as accidental, but not really. The Creature is both victim and perpetrator, which raises the harder question: how far does social rejection explain evil, and where does explanation stop short of justification?

## An Eve for Adam

At my current stopping point, the Creature has told Victor its story and asks him to create a female companion—an Eve to its Adam. It wants someone who looks like it and shares its exclusion, so that they can live apart together.

Victor initially agrees, but doubts are already beginning to creep in. I do not yet know how the conflict resolves.

## Preliminary verdict

Really good so far. The horror premise opens into questions about knowledge, parental obligation, education, appearance, social exclusion, companionship, and moral responsibility.
